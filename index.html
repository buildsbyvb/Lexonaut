Your sentence, chopped by each tokenizer

Each colored box is one token. The colors only help you see where one token ends and the next begins; they have no other meaning. A · at the start of a box means "there was a space before this piece". A ## at the start means "this piece is glued to the previous one".

What is fertility?

Fertility means tokens per word: number of tokens divided by number of words. Fertility 1.0 means one token per word. A higher number means words are being broken into more pieces, which is more work for the model and more cost for you.

Words become numbers

A computer cannot read meaning, so the AI turns every sentence into a list of 384 numbers called an embedding. Sentences that mean similar things get similar lists of numbers.

🌍 Real-world exampleWhen you search "cheap flights to Goa", the website turns your words into numbers and compares them with every page. A page titled "low-cost air tickets to Goa" still shows up, even though it shares almost no words with your search.
🎓 If your teacher asksAn embedding is a vector (a list of numbers) that represents a sentence. This project uses the model paraphrase-multilingual-MiniLM-L12-v2, which gives a 384-number vector for each sentence.

Same meaning = neighbours

Sentences with the same meaning sit close together on the map, even when they are written in different languages. The model learned this from a huge number of translated sentence pairs.

🌍 Real-world example"I am going to the market" in English, "मैं बाज़ार जा रहा हूँ" in Hindi and "मी बाजारात जात आहे" in Marathi land almost on the same spot, like three friends standing together. This is how a customer-care chatbot can answer a Hindi question using an English help page.
🎓 If your teacher asksThis is called cross-lingual semantic alignment: the meaning of a sentence decides its position, not its spelling or its language.

Why it matters

It lets computers search and compare by meaning instead of by exact words. This idea is behind modern search engines, chatbots, translation tools and recommendation systems.

🌍 Real-world exampleA help-desk bot can understand that "my internet is not working" and "WiFi is down" describe the same problem, and show the same fix for both. A music app can suggest songs with a similar feel in the same way.
🎓 If your teacher asksThis is called semantic search: instead of matching keywords, the system compares embeddings, usually with cosine similarity (the same score shown in the Style section as "Meaning kept").

Real sentences

Every dot on the map is a real sentence taken from a public translation dataset, in English, Hindi or Marathi. A filter hides unsuitable words so the map stays clean.

🌍 Real-world exampleThink of a phrasebook that a traveller carries: the same idea written in three languages, line by line. This map is built from thousands of such lines, so the dots are real everyday language, not examples invented by the developer.
🎓 If your teacher asksUsing real parallel (translated) data makes the map trustworthy, because the neighbours you see are formed by actual language use. It is a good point to mention when your teacher asks about the data source.

🗺️
Squashed to 3D

384 numbers are too many to draw, so a method called UMAP squashes them down to 3 so we can see them. Distances are therefore approximate, and your dot sits at the average of its 8 nearest sentences.

🌍 Real-world exampleIt is like flattening the round Earth onto a paper map: Greenland looks bigger than it really is, but cities that are close in real life still look close on the paper. In the same way, close dots on this map really are similar in meaning.
🎓 If your teacher asksUMAP is a dimensionality-reduction technique. It tries to keep neighbouring points together while reducing 384 dimensions to 3, which is why shape and clusters matter more than exact distances.
